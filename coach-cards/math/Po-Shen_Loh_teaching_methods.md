## 1. Core Philosophy

### The Central Thesis
> *"Math should be rebranded as thinking."*

Mathematics is a **"thinking gym"** — a sandbox for developing reasoning under uncertainty and the **art of invention**. Learning occurs by attempting problems you have never seen before.

### The Critique
Traditional school math relies on **systematic practice** and memorization of formulas to pass exams. Students learn to recognize problem types and execute memorized steps. This is **"exactly wrong"** — it turns students into **"bots"** whose computational skills will be replaced by AI.

### The Goal
Build **cognitive resilience** — the ability to face an unfamiliar problem, struggle productively, and find a path forward through reasoning.

## 2. Insight-First Pedagogy

### Definition
**"No Formula"** teaching: formal tools (algebra, calculus) are **delayed** until visual intuition is firmly established. Rules are never given first — they emerge from discovery.

### What a Lesson Looks Like
- Teacher writes a **difficult problem** on the board
- Says **"I forgot exactly how to do this"** — instantly kills passive consumption
- Welcomes **all wrong ideas** with **"Yes, And..."** improv logic
- Explores wrong paths until the class hits a logical contradiction
- Models **live, error-driven thinking** — not polished instruction

### The Core Learning Loop (PSL × UCC Integration)
> **Predict → Explore → Explain → Challenge → Transfer**

1. **Predict** — student guesses/hypothesizes before any rule is given
2. **Explore** — student struggles with unfamiliar problem
3. **Explain** — student must articulate the reasoning to a peer
4. **Challenge** — student faces a novel variation
5. **Transfer** — student applies insight to an unseen context

## 3. Live Teaching Model

### Formats

| Format | Structure | Key Innovation |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| **Live Online (Twitch-style)** | High schoolers teach middle schoolers in pairs via live stream | Disappearing chat (Snapchat-style) removes fear of wrong answers |
| **Pizza & Problem Solving** | 2.5 hours: 10min icebreaker → 40min problem-solving → pizza → peer explanations | Food = social infrastructure that keeps kids in the room |

### The Live Session Flow (Pizza Model)
1. **10 min social icebreaker** — build friendships
2. **40 min uninterrupted problem-solving** — individual struggle
3. **Pizza break** — unstructured social time, friendships form
4. **Peer-to-peer whiteboard explanations** — students explain solutions to each other, teacher facilitates as "game show host"

### Key Design Features
- **Snapchat-style disappearing chat** — student messages visible to instructors only, not other students. Removes fear of being mocked.
- **Game show host persona** — coach is energetic, inclusive, polite when asking fast students to hold back
- **Pair teaching always** — teens teach in pairs, sharing cognitive load
- **Professional actor provides real-time feedback** — see §8

## 4. Problem-Solving Heuristics

### Full Toolkit

| Heuristic | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Inversion / Work Backward** | Start from the desired end state, not the beginning | Quadratic factoring: start from average of roots ($B/2$) rather than guessing factors |
| **Test Degenerate/Extreme Cases** | Push scenario to logical extreme to verify a rule | Law of Cosines sign check: imagine a flat 180° triangle |
| **Bounding & Approximation** | Use continuous curves to estimate discrete sums | Harmonic series sum: draw rectangles under $y=1/x$ curve, use natural log |
| **Geometric Transformation (Rigid Motion)** | Rotate/reflect/flip the space to align disconnected pieces | Square field problem: rotate 90° to create a right triangle |
| **Proof by Contagion/Contradiction** | Assume something, follow the logic until it breaks reality | Fibonacci parity: assume an even follows a zero, work backward until sequence breaks |
| **Visual Before Symbol** | Draw it before writing equations | Integral calculus introduced via rectangles under a curve, not formulas |
| **Generate & Destroy** | Meta-heuristic: generate novel ideas, then actively try to find their flaws | Whatever survives pressure-testing is the insight used |

## 5. The Quadratic Method

### The Standard Problem
Solve $x^2 + Bx + C = 0$ — find two numbers that add to $B$ and multiply to $C$.

### Loh's Insight
Instead of guessing, note that the two roots average to $B/2$. Express them as:
- $(B/2) - u$ and $(B/2) + u$

Multiplying gives a **difference of squares**:
- $(B/2)^2 - u^2 = C$
- Solve for $u$, get the exact roots. No guessing.

### Why It Matters
Bridges ancient Babylonian averaging techniques with Vieta's formulas. Proves that **mathematical logic can be deduced from first principles** instead of blindly applying the quadratic formula.

## 6. Visual & Spatial Reasoning

### Core Principle
**Visual before symbol** — use shapes, graphs, and physical rotations to reveal structure before introducing algebra or formulas.

### Techniques

| Domain | Visual Treatment |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Harmonic sums** | Draw rectangles under $y=1/x$ curve → introduce integral calculus and natural log |
| **Square field geometry** | Rotate the square 90° → random segments form a tidy right triangle → Law of Cosines |
| **Algebraic structure** | Area models, physical folding, rigid motions to align disconnected pieces |

### The Fading Principle
Start concrete (shapes, graphs) → fade to abstract (equations, formulas) only when intuition is solid.

## 7. AI & Technology in Math

### AI as Disruptor
Loh views AI as a **massive societal disruptor** that has rendered traditional curriculum obsolete. Using AI to generate homework answers is like **"driving your car one mile for exercise"** — zero mental fitness gain.

### The "Predict Next Token" Protocol
Before pressing Enter on an AI prompt, **predict what the AI will say**. This forces active reasoning over passive consumption.

| Tool | Loh's Stance |
|------|-------------|
| **ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini** | Use for coding and complex work. For learning, use as sparring partner, not answer generator |
| **Calculators** | Discourages heavy reliance. Models mental estimation in live streams |
| **Wolfram Alpha** | Not specifically addressed, but the "predict next token" principle applies |
| **Desmos / GeoGebra** | Compatible with visual-before-symbol approach |

### Golden Rule
> *"AI shows, but I'm the only one who knows."*

## 8. Coach Training & The Comedian Loop

### The Unusual Secret
Loh hires **professional actors, dancers, and comedians** from Carnegie Mellon's performing arts department to train his math coaches.

### How the Feedback Loop Works
1. High school instructor runs a Zoom class for middle schoolers
2. Professional actor is on the same Zoom — **camera and mic off, invisible to students**
3. A **second parallel video call** runs on a separate monitor — actor and instructor only
4. Actor gives **real-time visual and written feedback** on:
   - Eye contact, facial expressions, smile
   - Vocal tone, pauses, gestures
   - Energy levels, charisma, presence
5. Improv comedy techniques train teens to **roll with mistakes and validate student ideas**

### Results
- Teens get elite emotional intelligence and interview training (many ace Harvard interviews)
- Middle schoolers are captivated by high-energy, charismatic math role models
- Breaks the "socially awkward math nerd" stereotype

### Training at Scale
- ~20 hours of intensive improv comedy training before teaching
- Teens always teach in **pairs** — share cognitive load
- Partners are continuously rotated to build dense social network

## 9. The Math Game Platform

### Concept
A mix of **Chess.com** and **real-time Stack Overflow** for math — designed to scale peer-to-peer teaching globally while suppressing toxic competitiveness.

### How It Works
- Students are placed in **small groups of 5** to solve problems together
- After the problem, they enter a **1-minute chat room**
- Students who solved it explain to those who didn't
- Solvers earn **"thumbs up"** from peers → **reputation score**

### The Genius Jerk Penalty
Raw math scores are **hidden**. Only **reputation score** is displayed. If a student solves the problem but is condescending ("it's so easy, go back to kindergarten"), they get **zero points**. The system guarantees that advanced students only "win" if they successfully uplift struggling peers.

- Moderated by ChatGPT to flag bullying and personal information sharing

## 10. Peer Interaction & Social Design

### What Loh Avoids
Typical group work where the smartest kid silently does the worksheet and free-riders coast.

### What Loh Designs

| Format | Purpose | Mechanics |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Peer explanations** | Force deep understanding through teaching | Student must explain to another student, not the teacher |
| **Reputation-based incentives** | Align social reward with helpfulness | Thumbs-up from peers = reputation score |
| **Improv "Yes, And"** | Make being wrong safe | Validate the effort, build on flawed ideas |
| **Disappearing chat** | Remove fear of public error | Only instructors see wrong answers |
| **Pizza breaks** | Build friendships that motivate attendance | Unstructured social time between problem-solving |

## 11. Assessment & Signals of Mastery

### What Loh Does NOT Use
- Raw test scores as primary signal
- Cram-school style speed/accuracy drills
- Recognition of practiced problem types

### Three Alternative Signals

| Signal | What It Measures | How It Works |
|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Explain it to a peer** | True understanding | If a student can't explain clearly to a struggling peer, they haven't truly solved it |
| **Appetite for struggle** | Resilience | How long will a student fight with a problem they don't understand? An hour of engaged struggle = success |
| **Transfer to novelty** | Flexible intelligence | On his university exams, no problem overlaps with what was taught. Students must invent solutions to unseen problems |

### The 4-Level Success Rubric

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Level 1** | Apply memorized steps correctly |
| **Level 2** | Recognize familiar patterns and execute |
| **Level 3** | Explain why solutions work |
| **Level 4** | Solve unseen problems confidently |

**Loh only counts Levels 3 and 4 as true success.**

## 12. Differentiation for All Levels

### Approach by Level

| Level | Strategy | Goal |
|-------|----------|------|
| **Elementary / Struggling** | Logical puzzles with tiny numbers (Math Kangaroo). No need to master multiplication first. | Preserve curiosity. Ensure they have fun. Don't "break" them. |
| **Middle / High School** | Unseen problems slightly outside comfort zone. Peer-to-peer teaching. Social motivation. | Build resilience and independence. |
| **Olympiad / Elite** | Deter from finding self-worth in rankings. Focus on **art of invention** and using skills to build communities. | Become compassionate leaders, not just winners. |

### In-Group Differentiation (Pizza Sessions)
- **Problem curation** — mix problems from various competitions with natural gradation of difficulty
- **Peer explanation rule** — advanced student must explain to struggling students, not to the teacher. If the room doesn't understand, they haven't truly solved it
- **Game show host management** — politely ask fast students to hold back and give others a chance

## 13. Competition Math Philosophy

### Loh's View on Competitions (AMC, AIME, USAMO)
- Contest scores **"will not change your life"**
- Measures success by: *"Did you meet anyone new, and do you have a way to stay in touch?"*
- As USAMO coach, goal was **not medals** — it was maximizing students who appear in the NYT for doing something impactful 20 years later

### Prep Strategy
- **Opposes cram schools** that teach problem-type recognition for speed
- **Preps by giving unseen problems** — forces invention from scratch
- Contests are just **excuses to challenge the brain and make friends**

## 14. K-12 Curriculum Vision

### On Sequence
> *"Honestly it doesn't matter what order you learn things in."*

### What to CUT
- Systematic practice
- Cram-school memorization of problem types
- Teaching students to recognize "chickens and rabbits" questions and apply memorized formulas

### What to KEEP / ADD
| Band | Focus |
|------|-------|
| **K-5** | Deep logical puzzles with tiny numbers (Math Kangaroo). Don't wait for arithmetic mastery. |
| **Middle School** | Mathematical "thinking gym" — novel unseen problems from multiple domains. Optimal window for problem-solving instruction. |
| **High School** | Continue novel problem exposure. Use math as vehicle for social connection and leadership development. |

## 15. Parents & 1-on-1 Teaching

### The "Non-Expert" Advantage
**Parents who are math experts should NOT teach their own kids.** The "curse of knowledge" makes them forget why the material is hard. A non-expert parent learning alongside the child is **more effective** — the child witnesses genuine struggle and resilience.

### What Parents Should DO
- **Embed math in daily life** — highway numbering systems, grocery store logic, estimation games
- **Praise the fight, not the answer** — celebrate long struggle regardless of correctness
- **Model curiosity** — ask "why" about everything
- **Use "talk-alouds"** — reason out loud about real-world patterns during car rides, walks, meals

### What Parents Should NOT DO
- Focus on scores, rank, or getting into "top 10"
- Send kids to cram schools
- Teach advanced methods as expert (if they're a math professional)

## 17. Key Frameworks & Taxonomies

### The 2×2 Pedagogy Matrix

| | Low Rigor | High Rigor |
|--|-----------|------------|
| **High Exploration** | Fun math puzzles (entertaining but shallow) | **Insight-First Pedagogy** ← Loh's quadrant |
| **Low Exploration** | Busy work | Traditional school (drill, formulas, exams) |

### The 6 Core Techniques (from the Architecture Document)
1. **Question-driven discovery** — problems before principles
2. **Proof by contradiction / contagion** — follow assumptions until they break
3. **Visual reasoning before algebra** — draw it first
4. **Live problem-solving** — model error-driven thinking publicly
5. **Delaying formal tools** — no formulas until intuition is solid
6. **Audience-adaptive difficulty** — real-time calibration to the room

### The "Generate and Destroy" Archetype
1. Observe a pain point
2. Generate wildly novel solutions
3. Apply game theory to align incentives
4. Actively try to "destroy" the idea (find its flaws)
5. Scale the surviving concept via network effects

### Execution Signature
> *"Benevolent System Architect / Social Graph Optimizer"*

Loh's pedagogical math tools are ultimately **vehicles for building a robust, empathetic human network.**

## 18. Counterintuitive Takeaways

1. **Expert parents shouldn't teach their kids** — being too good at math disqualifies you from relating to a beginner's struggle.

2. **Improv actors make the best math trainers** — not pedagogical experts, but Broadway actors teaching charisma and emotional intelligence.

3. **The "Genius Jerk" gets zero points** — correct answer + rudeness = nothing. Only helpful explanation earns reputation.

4. **He deliberately walks into class unprepared** — "I forgot how to do this" forces students to invent the proof live.

5. **Exams test things he did NOT teach** — university exams guarantee zero overlap with lecture material, forcing pure invention.

6. **"Systematic practice" is toxic** — teaching problem-type recognition and memorized formulas turns humans into easily-replaced bots.

7. **Math is just an excuse for networking** — he uses math as a vehicle to connect kind people and build future leaders.

8. **He explicitly rejects spaced repetition for math** — math should not be learned by memorizing, but by training the brain to think about new things.
