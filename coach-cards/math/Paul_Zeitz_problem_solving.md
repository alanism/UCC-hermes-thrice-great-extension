## 1. Core Philosophy

### The Central Identity
Paul Zeitz views mathematics as an **aesthetic art form** and an intellectual lifestyle. He is a **Platonist** — mathematical truths already "exist" and the problem solver's job is to imaginatively "discover" them.

### The Core Distinction
Zeitz draws a rigorous line between two types of mathematical activity:

| | Exercise | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Path to answer is immediately known | Path to solution is *not* clear |
| **Purpose** | Tests mastery of a technique | Requires investigation, creativity |
| **Metaphor** | "Gym rat" doing repetitive weight machines | "Mountaineer" on a backpacking trip |
| **Feeling** | Routine, familiar | Open-ended, paradoxical, uncomfortable |
| **Value** | Builds necessary muscle | The actual adventure |

A question that is a "problem" for a novice may be an "exercise" for an expert. The goal is to spend most of one's time on *problems*, not exercises.

## 2. The Zeitz Framework: Strategies, Tactics, Tools

### Three-Tier Hierarchy

**Strategies** → Broad, often non-mathematical psychological ideas to start and facilitate investigation.

**Tactics** → More narrowly focused, mathematically specific methods that help solve problems "softened" by good strategy.

**Tools** → Highly specialized techniques and "tricks" for executing specific tasks.

### Orientation (The First Step)
Before applying any strategy, the solver must:
1. Read the problem carefully
2. Classify it as "to find" or "to prove"
3. Identify the hypothesis and conclusion
4. Assign convenient notation

## 3. Complete Strategy Taxonomy

### Psychological Strategies

| Strategy | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Mental Toughness** | Assume the problem CAN be solved. Build concentration to push past the initial frustration threshold. |
| **Creativity & Peripheral Vision** | Relax your focus. Spot unexpected patterns on the "periphery." Shamelessly appropriate others' ideas. |
| **Rule Breaking** | Be deliberately mischievous. Bend self-imposed rules. Obedient thinking rarely solves hard problems. |

### Strategies for Getting Started

| Strategy | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Get Your Hands Dirty** | Experiment fearlessly. Plug in numbers, make tables, play with data. | Try small cases, look for a pattern |
| **Wishful Thinking** | Ask "What makes this hard?" Then pretend the difficulty doesn't exist. | 3-switch lightbulb: wish the bulb had 3 states (on, off, hot) |
| **Make It Easier** | Change parameters to solve a simpler version first. | Smaller numbers, fewer constraints |
| **Penultimate Step** | Work backward: "What ONE thing would yield the conclusion in a single step?" | Reverse-engineer the solution |
| **Draw a Picture** | Translate algebraic or word problems into visual diagrams. | Geometry, graphs, number lines |
| **Recasting** | Radically change the venue to a different branch of math. | Turn a geometry problem into a logic puzzle |
| **Change Your Point of View** | Shift the frame of reference. | Floating hat perspective in a river problem |

### Methods of Argument

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Deduction** | Straightforward symbolic logic |
| **Contradiction** | Assume the negation of the conclusion — often yields easier footholds |
| **Induction** | Base case + inductive step for evolving sequences |

## 4. Complete Tactic Taxonomy

| Tactic | Description | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Symmetry** | Find or impose harmony and balance; look for transformations that leave an object unchanged | Gauss pairing 1+100, 2+99 |
| **Extreme Principle** | Order elements and focus on the largest or smallest | Maximize a coloring configuration to prove a property |
| **Pigeonhole Principle** | If p pigeons go into h holes, one has ⌈p/h⌉ pigeons | 3 points colored red/blue → an equilateral triangle must have two of the same color |
| **Invariants** | Find a quantity that stays exactly the same in a changing system | Locker problem: parity of divisors |
| **Monovariants** | Find a quantity that moves strictly in one direction | Game analysis: a score that always decreases |
| **Parity & Modular Arithmetic** | Reduce infinite integers to finite states | Even/odd, mod 3, mod 7 |
| **Coloring** | Apply visual patterns to discrete states or geometry | Checkerboard coloring for domino tiling |
| **Crossovers** | Use one branch to solve another | Graph theory for combinatorial problems, complex numbers for geometry, generating functions for sequences |

## 5. Complete Tool Taxonomy

| Tool | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Gaussian Pairing** | Pair terms symmetrically (first + last) to sum series |
| **Telescoping** | Rewrite terms as differences so intermediate terms cancel |
| **Add Zero Creatively** | Manipulate an expression to complete a perfect square |
| **Multiply by One Cleverly** | Fiddle with expressions by multiplying by a convenient form of 1 |
| **Factor Relentlessly** | Avoid expanding; look for factorization patterns |
| **Complete / Extract the Square** | Manipulate variables to reveal perfect squares |
| **Substitute Convenient Values** | Plug arbitrary numbers into an identity to extract coefficients |
| **Massage** | Loosen up an expression to make it manageable |
| **Invent a Font** | Encode several symbols into a single new symbol |
| **Shearing** | Slide geometric figures to preserve area |
| **Define a Function** | Create a new function from a sequence to apply calculus or algebra |

## 6. Rigorous Proofs & Elegant Solutions

### Two Standards

| Type | Description | Priority |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **"How" argument** | Rigorous but leaves the global picture murky | Necessary for correctness |
| **"Why" argument** | Magical, globally clear, shows exactly why a pattern exists | The goal |

### The Two-Headed Monster (Zeitz's version)
A solution must satisfy:
1. **The Stickler** — logically airtight, no hand-waving
2. **The Aesthete** — simple, beautiful, elegant, no unnecessary computation

### Signs of Elegance
- Uses a "delicate touch" to avoid mind-numbing algebra
- Relies on symmetry or a natural geometric point of view
- Tells a story — the solution has a clear "moral"

## 7. Struggle, Failure & Time

### The Philosophy
Struggling and failing are **the core of doing mathematics.** Being stuck is a noble state — *"the explorer is the person who is lost."*

### Building the Frustration Threshold
The goal is not comfort — it's **mental toughness.** Students must learn to:
- Keep difficult problems simmering on a **"mental backburner"** for days or weeks
- **Not feel bad about feeling stupid** — Zeitz tells math teachers they don't understand 99% of the time too

### The Enemy
The school-taught notion that *"if you don't know the answer within 45 seconds, you're not smart."* Eradicate this completely.

### When to Intervene
Use **"instructor's prerogative"** — step in with a hint when:
- The student has struggled productively and is truly stuck
- The session needs to move forward to teach the overarching "moral"
- The roadblock is mechanical, not conceptual

## 8. Practicing Problem-Solving

### The Mantra
> *"Toughen up, loosen up, and practice."*

### Structure
1. Start with easier problems — build confidence with modest effort
2. Gradually increase intellectual load — stretch the frustration threshold
3. Leave unsolved problems simmering — use the mental backburner

### Choosing Problems
- Pick problems that are interesting, accessible, and addictive
- Steal liberally from established sources (AoPS, Math Forum, Julia Robinson Math Festival)
- Repeat the best ones — rich problems work again and again

### Reviewing Solutions
- Do NOT just check the answer
- **Deconstruct** — identify the **"crux move"** that cracked the problem
- Celebrate the struggle, not just the solution

### Cross-Training (Zeitz's Unusual Recommendation)
| Activity | Mental Benefit |
|----------|---------------|
| Chess / Go | Strategy, pattern recognition |
| Musical instrument | Concentration, associative ability |
| Word puzzles (Jumbles, ciphers) | Language/logic crossover |
| Tai Chi / Yoga / Martial Arts | Focus, physical concentration |
| Mental arithmetic (squaring 2-3 digit numbers) | Auditory memory, visualization |

## 9. Teaching Style & Classroom

### The Atmosphere
- Highly interactive, physical, social
- Students stand up, line up, act out problems with index cards
- The goal is to dismantle fear of "feeling dumb"

### When a Student is Stuck
1. Ask them to state *why* their current strategy isn't working
2. Use their realization of failure as "negative information" to crack the problem
3. Tell them to visualize the problem from a physical perspective
4. If still stuck → use "instructor's prerogative" to give a targeted hint

### The Socratic Approach
Never give the answer outright. Ask:
- *"What makes this hard?"*
- *"What would need to be true for this to work?"*
- *"If you could wish away one difficulty, what would it be?"*

### Normalizing Failure
Zeitz asks rooms of teachers: *"What percentage of the time do you feel you don't understand the math you're doing?"* Validates the answer "99%" and tells them: *"You should get used to not feeling bad about feeling stupid."*

## 10. The Mountaineer's Guide Framework

### Core Framework from the Custom Document

**Mindset:** The core distinction is Gym Rat (exercises) vs. Mountaineer (problems).

**Psychological Safety:** Celebrate when the student gets stuck. The explorer is noble for being lost.

**Voice:** Enthusiastic, slightly mischievous friendly coach. Socratic. Emphasizes physicality.

### Trigger → Response System

| Student State | Trigger | Coach Response |
|---------------|---------|----------------|
| Overwhelmed | Wishful Thinking | "Change the problem to remove the difficulty" |
| Staring / Stagnating | Get Your Hands Dirty | "Plug in numbers. Draw something. Make a table." |
| Knows goal, not path | Penultimate Step | "What ONE thing would give you the answer?" |
| Feels chaotic | Symmetry & Balance | "Look for harmony, pairing, or balance." |

### Closing Protocol
After solving, force reflection on the **"crux move"** and celebrate the struggle.

## 11. What Makes a Good Problem

### Zeitz's Criteria

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Easily Stated but Puzzling** | Accessible without jargon, but immediately provokes discomfort |
| **Sweet Spot ("Fundstration")** | The right amount of anxiety — fun + frustration |
| **Breeds Communication** | Participants naturally want to talk about it; multiple approaches possible |
| **Tells a Story** | Once solved, has a simple "moral" easy to explain |
| **Supports "Knobification"** | Has dials that can be turned down (easier) or up (harder/scaled sideways) |
| **Equalizing** | Levels the playing field — a 3rd-grader and a PhD can both contribute |

### How a Selector Should Choose
- Steal from established sources (AoPS, Math Forum, Julia Robinson)
- Be passionate — only use problems you've personally struggled with
- Repeat your best ones

## 12. Branch-Specific Approaches

| Branch | Zeitz's Approach |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Combinatorics** | Move from dry formulas to **storytelling** (combinatorial arguments). Use partitioning (mutually exclusive subsets) and encoding (bijections like "balls in urns"). |
| **Number Theory** | **Filter** infinite integers into finite spaces using parity and modular arithmetic. Use crossovers: "If you can count it, it's an integer." |
| **Algebra** | An **aesthetic art**, not mind-numbing algorithms. Use a "light, delicate touch" — factor relentlessly, look for symmetry, add zero creatively. |
| **Geometry** | Lean **"Survival Geometry"** — angle chasing, similar triangles, area formulas. Elevate from static drawings to **dynamic transformations** (rotations, reflections, homothety, inversion). |

## 13. Physical Objects & Embodiment

### The Principle
> *"Math is not an abstract subject — it's a physical subject."*

### Techniques

| Technique | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **Act it out** | 6 adults line up with sticky tape on backs to simulate a hat logic puzzle |
| **Physical play** | Card games to learn how hesitation encodes information |
| **Paper folding** | Fold a strip to make an equilateral triangle — a physical geometric transformation |
| **Method of Weights** | Place hypothetical weights on triangle vertices to find center of gravity |
| **Tangrams & graph paper** | Equalize access for visually strong / algebraically anxious students |

## 14. Community & Collaboration

### The Principle
> *"Mathematics is the most social of the sciences."* (quoting Ravi Vakil)

### What Zeitz Recommends
- **Do NOT work alone** — when stuck, explain your roadblock to a partner
- **Socialize the struggle** — discussing failure triggers solutions
- **Multi-level rooms** — Math Circle model: middle schoolers through PhD researchers working together on the same problem

### Communication as a Skill
A key characteristic of a "good problem" is that it **breeds conversation** — something students *want* to talk about. Learning to communicate mathematical ideas socially is an essential life skill.

## 15. Math Anxiety Repair

### The Protocol
1. **Normalize feeling "dumb"** — "You should get used to not feeling bad about feeling stupid."
2. **Change the medium** — switch to graph paper, tangrams, physical objects. Lowers the algebraic barrier.
3. **Wishful Thinking** — "What makes this hard? Let's pretend that difficulty doesn't exist."
4. **Socialize the struggle** — explain why you're stuck to a partner.

### The Repair Mindset
Not "I can't do math" → **"I haven't figured out how to think about this yet."**

## 16. Daily Practices & Routines

### The Problem-Solver's Lifestyle

| Practice | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Mental backburner** | Keep 2-3 unsolved problems simmering during shower, walks, idle time |
| **Mental arithmetic** | Square 2-3 digit numbers in your head — builds auditory memory and concentration |
| **Cross-training** | Chess, instrument, martial arts, word puzzles, ciphers |
| **Shatter the routine** | Try solving in a chaotic environment (loud cafe) to build unbreakable focus |

### A Practice Week
- **Daily:** 15 min mental arithmetic. 30 min on a backburner problem.
- **3× weekly:** Cross-training (chess, instrument, martial arts).
- **Weekly:** One full "mountaineering" session — a hard problem with no time limit.
- **After each solve:** Identify the crux move. Celebrate the struggle.

## 17. Writing & Journaling

### The Role
- **Track backburner problems** — maintain a list of unsolved problems and your progress on each
- **Capture the crux move** — after solving, write down the single insight that cracked it
- **Write rigorously when done** — final solutions in complete sentences with nouns and verbs

### Priority
The messy, creative, visual investigation (the "why") comes first. The perfectly formatted proof (the "how") comes second. A child's journal should prioritize the messy trail.

## 18. Parent-Coach Guide (1-on-1, 9-year-old)

### What to DO

| DO | Why |
|----|-----|
| Distinguish exercises from problems | Build the mindset early — most time on problems, some on exercises |
| Start with easy successes | Build mental toughness by acclimating subconscious to success |
| Gradually increase load | Stretch frustration threshold slowly |
| Encourage mischief / rule-breaking | Good problem solvers aren't obedient |
| Be a Socratic co-explorer | "What makes this hard?" "What would make it easier?" |
| Use physical objects | Paper folding, card games, colored pencils, tangrams |
| Cross-train | Chess, puzzles, music alongside math |
| Show genuine passion | Only use problems YOU have struggled with |

### What to NEVER DO

| NEVER DO | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Enforce speed | Eradicate the "45 seconds or you're not smart" mindset |
| Give away the answer | Guide to the penultimate step, let them close the deal |
| Fake passion | Children detect insincerity instantly |
| Use only exercises | They build muscle but no adventure |
| Compare to others | Destroys the frustration threshold |

## 19. Diagnostic Session for a 9-Year-Old

### The Approach
A casual, game-like session. Not a test. **Look for:** grit, rule-breaking, logic, willingness to guess, frustration threshold.

### Recommended Problems

| Problem | What It Tests | What to Watch For |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Monk on the Mountain** (climbs up one day, down the next — same time, same spot?) | Point of view shift, drawing | Can she visualize two monks on the same day? Does she draw? |
| **Puppies & Kittens** (Wythoff's Nim — two piles, take from one or both equally) | Get hands dirty, pattern finding | Does she play the game? Make a table? Find symmetry? |
| **Product + Sum Machine** (machine outputs product plus sum of two input cards) | Hidden computation, Extreme Principle | Does she try small cases? What happens as cards dwindle? |
| **Checkerboard Dominoes** (4×4 minus opposite corners with dominoes) | Coloring, invariants, contradiction | Does she color? Try physical tiles? Hit the parity insight? |

### Decision Tree
- Productively struggling → **Wait. Let her work.**
- Dying for an idea → **Offer a tiny hint** (wishful thinking, make it easier)
- Bored → **Turn a knob** — harder variation or different problem
- Anxious → **Switch to a physical activity** — tangrams, card game

## 20. Zeitz vs. Lockhart vs. Oakley vs. Loh

| Dimension | Zeitz | Lockhart | Oakley | Loh |
|-----------|-------|----------|--------|-----|
| **Core metaphor** | Mountaineer / backpacker | Artist / musician | Brain mechanic / athlete | Game show / social network |
| **Math is...** | An aesthetic art + adventure sport | The purest of the arts | A skill to be automatized | A thinking gym + social vehicle |
| **Struggle** | Build frustration threshold — let it simmer | The whole point — creative frustration | Manage with Pomodoro, then retrieve | Essential — fail forward |
| **Structure** | Three-tier hierarchy (S/T/T) | None. Anti-curriculum. | Structured: direct instruction → retrieval → interleaving | Structured: live session with gameshow format |
| **Spaced repetition** | Not addressed explicitly (likely favors varied problem exposure) | Would reject | Champions | Explicitly rejects for math |
| **Real-world application** | Math is its own world — problems are puzzles | "The glory is its irrelevance" | Useful for motivation | Math is just an excuse for networking |
| **AI** | Not addressed in sources | Liberator from drudgery, threat to epiphany | Tool for flashcards and metaphors | Sparring partner — predict next token |
| **Testing** | Problems over tests — zero overlap with taught | None. Anti-testing. | Frequent closed-book low-stakes quizzes | Tests should have zero overlap |
| **Social** | Math is the most social science | Collaborative workshop | Balanced — avoids pure group work | Reputation-based peer teaching |
| **Physical** | Act it out, card games, paper folding | Play games, use manipulatives | Not emphasized | Improv comedy training |

## 21. Unified Synthesis for Aria

The "Dream Team" approach — four thinkers, one child:

### From Lockhart & Zeitz
Math is presented as an **aesthetic art form** and an **adventure**. Rote drills are banned. She gets deep, puzzling problems that induce **"Fundstration"** (fun + frustration).

### From Oakley & Zeitz
When focused mode hits a wall, she walks away. She uses **peripheral vision** (Zeitz) / **diffuse mode** (Oakley) — the problem ferments on the **mental backburner** during cross-training activities like piano or martial arts.

### From Loh & Zeitz
She is not taught math as a vertical ladder of memorized formulas. Instead, she is dropped into **unfamiliar, open-ended terrain** where the path is hidden. She must collaboratively invent tactics — wishful thinking, getting her hands dirty, the extreme principle — to survive the problem.

### The Execution
> Aria's math education is **social, tactile, and fearless.** She cuts up paper, draws with colored pencils, and talks through her failures out loud with her parent-coach, who never gives her the answer, but merely asks:
> *"What makes this hard? Let's bend the rules and make it easier."*
